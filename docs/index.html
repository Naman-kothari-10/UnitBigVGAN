Multilingual Multi-Speaker Unit Vocoders

Naman Kothari1, Arjun Gangwar2, Adarsh Arigala2, S Umesh2

1 National Institute of Technology, Trichy  ·  2 Indian Institute of Technology, Madras

A systematic analysis of discrete speech representations across four Indian languages from two distinct families — Indo-Aryan (Bengali, Hindi) and Dravidian (Tamil, Telugu). We dissect how k-means cluster size (500 → 10k), continuous ECAPA-TDNN speaker conditioning, and auxiliary LID supervision influence intelligibility, speaker identity preservation, and cross-lingual unit sharing in an adapted BigVGAN vocoder.

Core findings at a glance

Discrete units extracted from self-supervised models entangle phonetic, speaker, and language cues. Evaluating all 20 vocoder configurations on unseen test speakers exposes three clear regimes:

Finding 01 · Granularity

Cluster size governs intelligibility

Scaling k-means from 500 to 10k yields monotonic Word Error Rate (WER) reductions across all four languages (e.g., Bengali 60.4% → 22.9%), driven by steady rises in PNMI and phoneme purity. Indo-Aryan languages outperform Dravidian by >20% absolute WER.

Finding 02 · Identity

Speaker vectors stop identity collapse

Unconditioned models (Units Only) suffer audible intra-utterance speaker and gender switching (SIM 0.16–0.21). Concatenating continuous 192-d ECAPA-TDNN embeddings boosts similarity 4–5× (up to 0.78), generalizing zero-shot to unseen speakers.

Finding 03 · Disambiguation

Language supervision helps at small k

At small cluster sizes (500, 1k), cross-lingual phonemes collapse into identical cluster IDs; language embeddings + auxiliary LID loss disambiguate them. At 10k, phonemes naturally separate, making explicit language supervision redundant.

Architecture & training pipeline

We adapt BigVGAN to reconstruct 16 kHz waveforms directly from discrete Data2Vec-AQC units rather than mel-spectrograms, optionally concatenating continuous speaker embeddings and language supervision.

SSL representation extraction

Extract 21st-layer embeddings from Data2Vec-AQC (pretrained on 30,000 h across 23 Indian languages) at a 20 ms hop (320 samples at 16 kHz), capturing high-level phonetic and semantic structure.

Unified multilingual k-means

Train five shared k-means codebooks (500, 1k, 2k, 5k, 10k) on 1,200 h of balanced speech across 22 Indian languages (IndicVoices, IndicTTS, Shrutilipi, SPRING-INX), then extract units for the 4 target languages.

Channel-wise conditioning

Map units via a learnable 128-d embedding table, then optionally concatenate repeated 192-d ECAPA-TDNN speaker vectors and/or 128-d language embeddings along the channel axis.

Adapted BigVGAN generator

Synthesize waveforms directly from X = Concat(L, S, U) on 26-unit training chunks (8,320 samples) and full-length unit sequences at inference, supervised by MPD & CQT-D discriminators (LSGAN + FM + L1 mel).

Auxiliary LID supervision

A lightweight 2-layer Conv1D + ReLU + LayerNorm classifier predicts language ID from mel-spectrograms; its cross-entropy loss on generated audio (λ_LID = 1) discourages cross-lingual accent drift.

Multi-axis evaluation

Trained 400k steps (batch 64, 4× A100 GPUs). Evaluated on 16 unseen speakers/lang via Indic-Conformer 600M (WER), VERSA ECAPA-TDNN cosine similarity (SIM), and IndicMFA (PNMI & purity).
